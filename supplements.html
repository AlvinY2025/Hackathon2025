Supplement Roadmap (Evidence-Based)

A clean, science-backed overview of the few supplements that actually work, who should take them, and why they matter.

The Only Supplements Worth Considering

Whey Protein

Best For: Anyone struggling to hit daily protein intake.

Science: Whey is fast-digesting and rich in leucine, the amino acid most responsible for triggering muscle protein synthesis. It is as effective as whole food protein but more convenient.

Benefits: Builds muscle, preserves muscle during fat loss, easy post-workout nutrition.

Why It Works: Research consistently shows that hitting total protein intake is one of the strongest predictors of muscle growth.

Creatine Monohydrate

Best For: Lifters wanting more strength, muscle fullness, and better training performance.

Science: Creatine increases phosphocreatine stores in muscle, enabling more high-power training and faster ATP regeneration. It is one of the most studied supplements ever.

Benefits: More reps before fatigue, increased strength, enhanced recovery, cognitive benefits.

Why It Works: Meta-analyses consistently find creatine increases training quality and muscle growth, especially in compound lifts.

Caffeine

Best For: People who train early in the morning or struggle with workout intensity.

Science: Caffeine blocks adenosine receptors and increases dopamine signaling, improving energy, focus, and perceived exertion.

Benefits: Better performance, alertness, endurance, and mental drive.

Why It Works: Research confirms caffeine reliably enhances strength, endurance, and mental performance — making it a simple but powerful pre‑workout ingredient.

Vitamin D

Best For: Anyone living in northern climates or indoor lifestyles.

Science: Vitamin D influences hormone function, bone health, immune regulation, and mood. Deficiency is extremely common.

Benefits: Improved recovery, energy levels, immune health, and strength.

Why It Works: Studies show that people deficient in vitamin D experience reduced strength and energy — supplementation fixes that.

Omega‑3 Fish Oil

Best For: Individuals who eat little fish or want joint, heart, and inflammation support.

Science: EPA and DHA reduce inflammation, support heart health, and improve joint function, especially for active people.

Benefits: Better recovery, reduced joint pain, cardiovascular health.

Why It Works: Research shows omega‑3 intake correlates with improved recovery, lower inflammation markers, and reduced delayed muscle soreness.

Supplements You Don't Need

The majority of supplements are unnecessary. Fat burners, BCAAs, testosterone boosters, and most pre‑workout ingredients besides caffeine show little to no benefit in large-scale studies.
